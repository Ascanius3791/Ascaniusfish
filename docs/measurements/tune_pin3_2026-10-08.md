## The three directions

Checked against `src/basic_eval.cpp` and `src/king_safety.cpp`; no eval source is touched.

1. **Passed pawn on the 7th vs the pawn table's 7th rank.** Every pawn on the 7th is passed
   (nothing stands on rank 8 to block its files), so `passed_pawn_value[6]` is a constant on
   rank 7 of the pawn endgame table. Pinned: bonus 0, moved into the 8 table squares, before
   the table-mean pin, so the mean (ranks 2-7) then moves into `piece_value_endgame[0]` and
   both constraints hold after the rewrite. **The opening bonus has the same identity but is
   not a null direction in the pinned gauge**: the held pawn opening value (the unit) breaks
   it. With only the #104 pins its SE at lambda 0 is 9.9 cp, and the two exact null
   directions the fit reported sit in the endgame. I first pinned it too (valid loss
   0.112984 instead of 0.112954, the unit moved to 151); that was one real constraint too
   many and is gone.
2. **Knight.** `m = count(a & ~own_pieces) = |a| - count(own_pieces & a)`, `a` the square's
   knight attacks, so `D` per defended piece (`activity_knight_defend`) equals `-D*m` on both
   mobility tables plus `D*|a(sq)|` on both knight tables; both blend with weights summing to
   `PHASE_MAX`, so only the final truncation differs. Exact. Pinned: `activity_knight_defend = 0`.
3. **`ks_shelter` constant vs material.** *Exact in the eval except above a cap.* The penalty is
   `penalty * min(M, 12) / 12`, `M = N + B + 2R + 4Q` of the enemy, and three files each add
   one `ks_shelter` entry (centre clamped to b..g). A constant `d` on all entries is `d*M/4`
   per king, i.e. knight and bishop `d/4`, rook `d/2`, queen `d` in both phases: the SE
   ratio R : B : Q = 2 : 1 : 4 of the old doc is the weights of `M`. 12 is the whole army, so
   the cap bites only after a promotion. The fit's weakest direction before the pin shows it:
   `-0.32 piece_value_endgame[4], +0.32 ks_shelter[0,2..7]` (Rayleigh 2.2e-13, not 1e-23,
   because of the cap and the penalty's truncation). Pinned: `d = ks_shelter[2]` (a king behind
   its home pawn) rounded to a multiple of 4 (108 on w8), taken off all 8 entries and added to
   the four piece values; `ks_shelter[2]` (now -1) is then held. `ks_shelter[1]` has no data
   and moves with the rest.

## Rewrite check

`basic_eval()` of the set as read against the rewritten one, on all 3,064,881 positions:

| gauge | differ on | mean | max | beyond 4 cp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pin104 | 1,075,750 | 0.392 | 4 | 0 |
| pin (all three) | 1,294,545 | 0.481 | 109 | 161 |

The 161 are the cap: a side with more than 12 units of material (promotions), where the
shift `d*M/4` should stop growing; 108 * 4 / 4 = 108. That is 5e-5 of the data; the model has
the same cap and is exact. Elsewhere mean and max are at rounding level. If a max of 4
everywhere is wanted, the alternative is to hold one `ks_shelter` entry at its current value
without rewriting (nothing moves, but the gauge value is whatever the set has). Not done.

## Reading

The material levels now have finite, small SEs at lambda 0 (queen 13-15, the rest under 7).
The lambda 0 and 1e-9 value sets are still far apart (queen opening 451 against 1336, 3852
against 2850 in the endgame), with SEs (13 and 5.6 cp) that are far smaller than that gap. So
the gap is not a gauge and not statistical noise by these SEs: the loss gain from 1e-9 to 0
(0.00037 valid) is bought by moving along directions that the quadratic model calls well
determined. Why those sets play worse (#104: -60 ± 32 Elo) is not answered here; the SEs are a
lower bound (positions of one game share a result). No SPRT, and no set becomes a
`weights/wN.txt`.
