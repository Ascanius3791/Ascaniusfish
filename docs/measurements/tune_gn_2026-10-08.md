**Adam was not stopping short.** Refit 3's valid loss stops moving at epoch ~250 (97 s
into the run) and stays at 0.113766 for the remaining 1250 epochs. Each run does reach the
minimum of its objective; what moves from run to run is the objective. lambda pulls the
weights toward the `from` set, so a fit from its own result has its anchor moved and falls
again. The restarts gain 0.00054, 0.00027, 0.00017 (valid): a walk toward the minimum of a
weaker lambda. lambda 1e-11 shows where it leads: valid −0.00066 against w7 in one run, but
with the queen's opening value at 272 (w7 1313, endgame 2284 → 3408) and the knight's at 98.
That is the direction the w7 doc already saw (opening values fall, endgame values rise),
and the data hold it only weakly.

So the method choice is about time, not the result: GN takes 3-4 steps of ~10 s, Adam 1500
epochs of ~0.26 s (it would be done at ~250). GN needs ~40 MB more (one 1058² matrix per
thread). How far the fit may move from the previous set is lambda's question, not the
optimiser's.

## Standard errors

At w7 (`iters=0`): Cov(w) ≈ σ²/n · (J'J/n + lambda)⁻¹, σ² = 0.11384 the mean squared
residual, n = 2,455,995. Treating the positions as independent (a game gives several, all
with one result) makes these a lower bound. SE* is gauge-free: a table or mobility entry
against its table's mean, a piece value plus the means of its tables, since a constant moved
between them leaves the eval unchanged and only lambda decides where it sits. lambda is
itself a prior of sd √(σ²/(n·lambda)) = **6.8 cp** around `from`; "lambda-held" counts the
weights whose SE* shrinks by more than 1.5 when lambda is ×10, i.e. the prior gives more than
about a seventh of their precision.

## The gauge pinned (df05613)

`tools/tune` now fixes the directions that leave the eval alone (`gauge=pin`, the default;
`gauge=free` is the old behaviour):

- every square table's mean is 0 in both phases, over the squares the piece can stand on
  (pawns: ranks 2-7). The king's constant cancels (one king a side) and is dropped;
- every mobility table is 0 at its most frequent count (R 5, N 6, B 7, Q 9; on all of
  tune2's train the queen's is 10, by a little). I chose this over the mean entry: the mean
  weighs a queen with 27 squares like one with 9, and the rare entries are the noisy ones.
  This way a piece value is the value of a piece with typical mobility on an average square;
- the pawn's opening value stays the held unit. It takes its table's mean
  (w7: 140 → 137) and is fixed from then on.

The solver drops one square per table (its anchor moves by minus the others' steps). Adam
takes each step's mean off every table. Weights no train position touches (54) are held at
`from`, so `lambda=0` runs.

**The rewrite of w7 changes no model eval.** The real `basic_eval()` changes on 1,072,996 of
3,064,881 positions (all three splits): mean |diff| 0.385 cp, max 4 cp. Three separate
`/PHASE_MAX` truncations do this (material, square tables, mobility each truncate apart), so
moving an integer between them can move a truncation. The model has no truncation. w7's
losses after the rewrite: 0.113840 / 0.113938 / 0.113744 (test +0.000001).

### Standard errors in the pinned gauge

At lambda 1e-9 the group medians match the free gauge's SE* to ~0.1 cp. The exception is the
mobility tables, now measured against the pinned count (knight opening 1.96 against 1.67). SE* = SE now,
and the piece values' SE is the SE of X: rook 3.3 / 3.5, knight 2.3 / 2.5, bishop 2.5 / 2.5,
queen 5.7 / 5.4, pawn endgame 1.3 (opening / endgame). All but the pawn's are lambda-held.
Against w6 (rewritten too): 523 of 996 changes exceed 2 SE, 359 exceed 3.

At lambda 0 the SEs say what the data alone determines. **J'J is still singular**, with two
exact null directions (Rayleigh quotient ~1e-23 against a largest diagonal of 1.4e-6):

1. **Pawns on the 7th rank**: every one is passed, so `passed_pawn_value[6]` (endgame)
   duplicates the rank-7 constant of the pawn endgame table.
2. **Knight mobility**: a knight's count is m = (knight moves from its square) − (own pieces
   it defends). So s·m on the knight mobility tables, −s·(move count) on its square tables
   and +s on `activity_knight_defend` leave every eval alone. A slider's attacks depend on the
   board, so only the knight has this.

The SEs therefore take the smallest ridge that lets Cholesky through, 1e-15 × the largest
diagonal. The fit itself needs none, because LM's damping keeps every step defined. The SEs
along those two directions are the ridge's: ~1e5 for the knight's tables, mobility, value and
`activity_knight_defend`, and for the pawn endgame table and value. The next direction is
near-degenerate rather than exact (1.7e-13): **the king-shelter constant against material**.
The shelter penalty is scaled by the enemy's phase material, so a constant on `ks_shelter`
trades against piece values in proportion to their phase units. It sets the other values'
SEs at lambda 0, in ratio 2 : 1 : 4:

| at lambda 0 | SE (cp) |
|---|---|
| rook X, opening / endgame | 83 / 83 |
| bishop X | 41 / 42 |
| queen X | 165 / 168 |
| `ks_shelter` (median) | 166 |
| R B Q square tables (median, opening / endgame) | 6.0 / 6.0, 4.8 / 7.2, 6.9 / 13.9 |
| king tables | 32 / 10 |
| queen mobility | 4.8 / 12.0 |
| threats by pawns | 18.8 |
| everything else | within ~1.5× of lambda 1e-9 |

So without lambda the data determine the square tables and most terms to a few cp, but not
the material levels. Those are fixed only up to the shelter trade and, for knights and pawns,
the two exact identities. Pinning one `ks_shelter` entry, one knight mobility slope and
`passed_pawn_value[6]` would remove all three. Not done here.
